# Recording guide

How to record the Capstone video: the tool, the setup, what to show, and what to say.

**ICAI requirement (AICA_Level2_Ver1.pdf, page 5):** the video must include **both the
participant's facial expression and the technical content**. Upload to YouTube **unlisted**
or Google Drive, with the link **accessible to anyone**, and submit it through the same
Google Form as the ZIP.

Target length: **8 to 10 minutes.**

---

## PART 1 — Which tool

### Recommended: Zoom

You already have it and already know it from the course. It records your face and your
screen together in one file, with no configuration.

1. Open Zoom → **New Meeting** (camera on)
2. **Share Screen** → choose the **browser window**, not the whole desktop
   (sharing one window means a stray notification on another screen cannot appear)
3. Tick **Share sound** only if you need it — you do not
4. Your camera thumbnail floats over the share. **Drag it to the bottom-right corner**
5. **Record → Record on this Computer**
6. Speak. When finished: **Stop Record → End Meeting**
7. Zoom converts the recording to MP4, usually into `Documents\Zoom\<date>\`

### Alternative: Clipchamp (built into Windows 11)

Record & Create → **Screen and camera**. Produces a picture-in-picture layout. Slightly
better looking than Zoom, slightly more fiddly.

### Alternative: OBS Studio (free)

Most control, most setup. Only worth it if you already use it.

### Do NOT use

- **Xbox Game Bar (Win+G)** — screen only, no webcam. Fails the ICAI requirement.
- **PowerPoint screen recording** — no webcam overlay. Same problem.
- **Phone pointed at the monitor** — unreadable.

## PART 2 — Before you press record

### Confidentiality — do this first

- [ ] Close **every** window with client data: Tally, Excel, email, WhatsApp Web, the
      income-tax portal
- [ ] Close every other browser tab. Bookmarks bar too — client names live there
- [ ] Sign out of any client portal in the browser you are about to share
- [ ] Use **only the twelve synthetic sample cases**. Never type a real client name
- [ ] Turn on **Focus assist / Do not disturb** (Win + N → Focus)

### Readability

- [ ] Browser at **full screen** (F11 toggles it off again if it hides the tab bar you need)
- [ ] Press **Ctrl + `+`** two or three times to zoom to about **125%**. Text that is
      comfortable on your monitor is unreadable once the video is compressed and watched
      in a small window
- [ ] Switch the app to **Executive Bright** mode (the third theme button). White ground,
      high contrast, compresses cleanly. Dark mode looks good live and muddy on video
- [ ] Record at **1080p** if your tool offers a choice

### Audio — the most common failure

- [ ] Use a **headset or earphone mic**, not the laptop's built-in microphone
- [ ] Quiet room. Close the window. Fan off
- [ ] **Record 20 seconds, stop, and listen back before doing the real take.** More capstone
      videos are spoiled by unusable audio than by anything else

### Camera

- [ ] Light in front of you, not behind. A window behind you turns you into a silhouette
- [ ] Camera at eye level — stack books under the laptop if needed
- [ ] Plain background
- [ ] Keep the webcam overlay on **for the whole recording**, not just the start. The
      requirement says facial expression, and an assessor skipping to the middle should
      still see you

### Your script

Put it on a **second monitor, a tablet, or a printed sheet beside you** — never on the
screen you are sharing. Reading from the shared screen is obvious and looks bad.

## PART 3 — What to show, and what to say

Follow **[`demo-script.md`](demo-script.md)** — it has the spoken lines for each beat. The
click path is:

| Time | Beat | On screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Introduce yourself, the firm, the project | Your face |
| 0:45 | The problem — and the 2023 change | Face / Overview |
| 1:15 | The solution; point at the green "Legal basis" strip | Overview panel |
| 1:45 | **Your own test data, typed live** — Reset, then turnover ₹1 crore, all receipts/payments/cash 0, profit and income ₹5 lakh | Financial Inputs → Decision |
| | "One more answer needed" → **Go to** → answer the bar question **No** → answer prior use **No** (NOT APPLICABLE) → change to **Yes** (APPLICABLE, s. 44AB(e)) | Decision / Legal Tests |
| 3:00 | Cash tests and the ₹10 crore limit | **Case 3** → Legal Tests |
| 3:45 | A reasoned "not applicable" | Decision |
| 4:30 | **The Finance Act 2023 first proviso** — ₹2.5 crore trader outside s. 44AB | **Case 4** → Section References |
| 5:30 | **The error verification caught** — professional below 50%, first year | **Case 8** |
| 6:30 | The five-year bar | **Case 10** |
| 7:00 | Generate the working paper and one-pager | Working Paper panel |
| 7:45 | **AI and human review** — what was checked, what was found | Section References / sources |
| 9:00 | Close | Your face |

**The two beats that carry the marks:**

**Beat 04 — typing your own data live.** It shows the tool asking only what the law needs,
taking you straight to each question, and a single fact flipping the answer. Rehearse the
clicks once.

**Beat 08 — the error.** Saying plainly that your first version got s. 44AB(d) wrong, and
that checking the statute caught it, is the strongest evidence of human-in-the-loop you can
give. Don't hide it.

## PART 4 — How to speak

**Slow down.** Nervous people speed up. Consciously go slower than feels natural — it will
sound normal on playback.

**Pause instead of saying "um".** Silence reads as thinking. Filler reads as uncertainty.

**Do not read the screen aloud.** The assessor can see "₹8,20,00,000". Say what it *means*:
"turnover of ₹8.2 crore, which is within the ₹10 crore limit."

**Speak to one person.** Imagine explaining it to a colleague across the desk, not addressing
a hall.

**Use your hands and the cursor.** Move the mouse to what you are talking about. A cursor
drifting aimlessly is distracting; a cursor resting on the figure you are discussing helps.

**It is fine to restart.** Do the whole thing in one take if you can. If you stumble badly,
stop, pause three seconds, and repeat the sentence — most tools let you trim, and an assessor
will forgive a small edit far more readily than a rambling ten minutes.

**Own the error you found.** Saying that verification caught a mistake in your first version
is the clearest proof that a human checked the law. It strengthens the project.

## PART 5 — After recording

1. **Watch it back completely.** Check: audio audible throughout? Face visible throughout?
   Text readable? Any client data anywhere on screen?
2. **Check the length.** 8 to 10 minutes.
3. Upload:

   **YouTube (recommended):** youtube.com → Create → Upload video → set visibility to
   **Unlisted** → copy the link.

   **Google Drive:** upload → right-click → Share → **Anyone with the link** → Viewer →
   copy link.

4. **Test the link in a private browsing window** (Ctrl + Shift + N). If it asks you to sign
   in or request access, the sharing setting is wrong and the submission will not be assessed.
5. Paste the link into `demo/video-link.txt`.
6. Submit it on the Google Form alongside the ZIP.

- [ ] Face visible throughout
- [ ] Audio clear, tested before the real take
- [ ] Browser zoomed to ~125%, Executive Bright mode
- [ ] `app/index.html` opened and driven live
- [ ] Only synthetic sample cases used
- [ ] No client data visible in any window, tab or bookmark
- [ ] Your own data typed live; Case 3, Case 4 (2023 proviso), Case 8 (the error caught) and Case 10 (the bar) shown
- [ ] Working paper generated on camera
- [ ] AI boundary and the legal verification addressed directly
- [ ] 8 to 10 minutes
