# Section 44AD — Presumptive taxation, eligible business

**Period:** FY 2025-26 / AY 2026-27 · Verified 18 September 2026 · [`sources.md`](sources.md)

---

## Why it matters for audit

1. **The first proviso to s. 44AB** (Finance Act 2023) takes a person who declares under
   s. 44AD(1) outside s. 44AB altogether — even with turnover above ₹1 crore.
2. **Section 44AB(e)** requires audit where s. 44AD(4) applies and total income exceeds the
   basic exemption — with no turnover threshold.

## Who can use it

A **resident individual, HUF or partnership firm (not an LLP)** — e-filing portal, ITR-4 FAQ.
Not available to: a business earning **commission or brokerage**, an **agency business**
(s. 44AD(6)), a **goods-carriage business** under s. 44AE, or an assessee claiming deductions
under ss. 10A, 10AA, 10B, 10BA or Chapter VI-A Part C.

## The ceiling — Finance Act 2023, s. 16 (Gazette text)

> *"Provided that where the amount or aggregate of the amounts received during the previous
> year, in cash, does not exceed five per cent. of the total turnover or gross receipts of such
> previous year, this sub-clause shall have effect as if for the words "two crore rupees", the
> words "three crore rupees" had been substituted: Provided further that for the purposes of
> the first proviso, the receipt of amount or aggregate of amounts by a cheque drawn on a bank
> or by a bank draft, which is not account payee, shall be deemed to be the receipt in cash."*

**The test is cash receipts against turnover only.** Cash payments play no part — unlike the
s. 44AB(a) ₹10 crore test, which looks at both receipts and payments against total receipts
and total payments.

## Deemed income

8% of turnover, reduced to **6% for the portion received by account-payee cheque, draft,
ECS or prescribed electronic mode during the year or before the s. 139(1) due date**. The
deemed amount is computed by component:

```
deemed = 8% × turnover not received through banking channels by the due date
       + 6% × the rest
```

It always lies between 6% and 8% of turnover. The engine asks for the split only when
declared profit falls in that band.

## Section 44AD(4) — opting out, and the five-year bar

Where s. 44AD was used for a year and, in any of the next five years, profit is declared
below the deemed rate, the assessee is barred from s. 44AD for the five following assessment
years, and s. 44AB(e) requires audit where total income exceeds the basic exemption.

The engine asks two questions, each only when it matters:

- **Is AY 2026-27 within a five-year bar?** If so, audit applies in this year wherever total
  income exceeds the exemption — **whatever the turnover** (the literal and conservative
  reading, reported from the ICAI Guidance Note, Revised 2026).
- **Was s. 44AD used in any of AY 2021-22 to AY 2025-26?** Asked only where profit is below
  the deemed income and income exceeds the exemption. If yes, declaring less now attracts
  s. 44AD(4) and audit.

The verbatim text of s. 44AD(4) and (5) was not retrieved for this project. Read it from the
bare Act.

## Basic exemption

Individual / HUF: ₹4,00,000 (new regime, FA 2025) or ₹2,50,000 (old regime); resident
individuals aged 60–79 ₹3,00,000 and 80+ ₹5,00,000. **A firm has none** — any positive income
exceeds it.
