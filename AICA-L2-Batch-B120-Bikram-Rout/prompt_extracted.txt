Prompt for BRS Tool:
Step  1
Build a complete web application named BRS Tool for bank reconciliation across more than 300 bank accounts, multiple companies, multiple banks, and multiple currencies.
Act as a senior full-stack developer, reconciliation specialist, and finance dashboard designer. Deliver a working application with persistent storage, document processing, reconciliation logic, dashboards, reports, and approval workflows. All buttons and workflows must function.
1. Business objective
Users upload:
Bank ledgers exported from Oracle Accounting System.
Bank statements downloaded from different banks.
The application must extract, validate, standardize, and reconcile transactions, identify outstanding items and variances, and generate account-wise Bank Reconciliation Statements.
Support monthly reconciliation, year-to-date analysis, historical comparisons, and consolidated management dashboards.

5. Standardized transaction data
Normalize ledger and statement records into:

Description and counterparty.
Reference, cheque number, payment ID, and Oracle journal/batch ID.

Currency.

Source document and source row/page.
Import batch ID.
Validation status.

Use exact decimal arithmetic or integer minor units for financial calculations. Do not use binary floating-point arithmetic for money.
Normalize both sources into a common cash perspective:

6. Import validation and duplicate controls
Validate:
Missing mandatory fields.
Invalid or ambiguous dates.
Incorrect amounts and currency.
Account and company mismatches.
Transactions outside the selected period.
Statement opening balance + signed movements = closing balance.
Ledger opening balance + signed movements = closing balance, where complete source data is available.
Gaps and overlaps in statement coverage.
Multiple currencies in a single account upload.
OCR uncertainty and unparseable rows.
Detect duplicate files using file hashes.
Identify potential duplicate transactions using available source identifiers and transaction attributes. Identical date/amount/description combinations can represent legitimate repeated payments; flag them for review rather than automatically deleting them.
Make re-imports idempotent. Replacing a batch must require confirmation and preserve its audit history.
Show imported, rejected, flagged, and duplicate-candidate record counts. Never silently discard records.
7. Reconciliation engine
Reconcile within the same company, bank account, and currency.
Implement configurable matching rules in this order:
Exact unique reference and exact amount.
Exact cheque/payment/journal reference and amount.
Exact amount with configurable date tolerance and supporting reference evidence.
Normalized reference/description with exact amount and date tolerance.
One ledger transaction to multiple bank transactions.
Multiple ledger transactions to one bank transaction.
Controlled many-to-many matching with exact net amounts and bounded group sizes.
Fuzzy descriptions as suggested matches requiring review.
Default amount tolerance must be zero. Allow approved, currency-specific tolerance settings.
Do not mark tolerance differences as fully reconciled without a separately identified and approved adjustment.
Include:
Configurable date tolerance by account or rule.
Matching across month boundaries for outstanding items.
Rule priority and versioning.
Match confidence, explanation, and supporting evidence.
Exact sum validation for grouped matches.
Configurable limits on group size and search window.
No reuse of a transaction in another match.
Transaction locking and atomic updates to prevent concurrent double matching.
Ambiguous matches routed to review.
Manual matching, partial allocations, and unmatching with mandatory reasons.
For partial allocations, track the allocated and remaining amount explicitly; prevent allocation above the original transaction amount.
Only unique, approved-rule deterministic matches may be automatically confirmed. AI-assisted or fuzzy suggestions require human confirmation.
Statuses should include Unmatched, Suggested Match, Matched, Partially Matched, Exception, and Carried Forward.

9. Outstanding items and period management
Classify outstanding items as:
Outstanding payments or cheques.
Deposits in transit.
Bank charges not recorded in the ledger.
Bank interest not recorded in the ledger.
Direct debits or credits.
Timing differences.
Possible duplicates.
Reversals.
Amount differences.
Unidentified transactions.
Potential ledger or bank errors.
Support configurable aging buckets such as 0–30, 31–60, 61–90, and over 90 days.
Carry unresolved items into subsequent periods without duplicating them. Preserve their original date and history. When cleared later, record the clearing date and period.
Maintain immutable approved period snapshots. Later clearance of an item must not change a previously approved report.
Period workflow:
Draft → In Progress → Submitted → Reviewed → Approved → Locked.
Reopening a locked period requires permission, a reason, and an audit entry.
10. Bank Reconciliation Statement
For each account and period, display:
Opening and closing bank statement balances.
Opening and closing Oracle ledger balances.
Raw closing balance difference.
Matched transaction counts and amounts.
Ledger-only outstanding items.
Bank-only outstanding items.
Approved adjustments.
Adjusted bank balance.
Adjusted ledger balance.
Residual unreconciled difference.
Build the reconciliation using documented cash-sign conventions.
For validated timing items:
Adjusted bank balance = bank statement closing balance + signed ledger-only movements not yet reflected by the bank.
Adjusted ledger balance = ledger closing balance + signed bank-only movements not yet recorded in the ledger.
Treat errors and other reconciling adjustments explicitly with reviewed signs and explanations. Do not apply the timing-item formula indiscriminately to every unmatched item.
Require:
Each reconciling amount to link to supporting transaction evidence.
Separate treatment of opening/prior-period differences.
No double counting of carry-forward items or adjustments.
A visible residual difference.
Clear identification of missing or invalid source balances.
Never force the residual to zero or create a balancing adjustment automatically. Reconciliation is complete only when required validations pass, the residual is zero at the account currency precision, and approvals are completed.
Proposed journal entries are suggestions for review only. Do not post entries to Oracle automatically.
