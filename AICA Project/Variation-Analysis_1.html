Methodology

Unit of analysis: a "recurring task type" is one (Client, Service) pair — e.g. "7Span Internet Pvt Ltd — GSTR-1". Each distinct task created for that pair (one per filing period — a month, quarter, or year, per SharePoint's Task list) is one instance. An instance's "hours" is the sum of every Time Sheet entry logged against that specific task, across everyone who worked on it.

Only recurring types are analyzed: a (Client, Service) pair needs at least instances (periods) to be included — a one-off engagement has nothing to compare against, so it's excluded entirely rather than flagged.

Flag criteria: a task type is flagged as showing "substantial variation" if its coefficient of variation (CV = standard deviation ÷ mean, across instance hours) is at least , or its highest instance is at least × its lowest instance. Either test alone is enough — CV catches broad noisy spread, the ratio test catches a single extreme outlier period that CV can understate when most other periods happen to cluster together.

Routine compliance vs. variable-scope: GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-9, TDS Return Filing, TDS Payment, GST Registration, and Income Tax Return Filing are mechanical periodic filings where the scope of work is nearly identical every period — variation there is the most actionable signal, since there's no obvious reason a GSTR-1 for the same client should take 10× longer some months. Bookkeeping, Company Audit, Litigation, Income Tax Audit, Review Report, GSTAT, and "Other" are inherently variable in scope (transaction volume, case complexity, audit findings) — variation there is expected and shown separately rather than flagged with equal weight. The toggle at the top switches the whole report between these two views.

Staff "vs. peer average": for each (Client, Service) task type a person has worked on at least twice, this compares their own average hours-per-instance against that task type's overall average across everyone who's touched it (including the person themselves) — a positive % means they typically log more hours than the group average on shared task types, negative means fewer. This isn't automatically good or bad: it can reflect thoroughness, inefficiency, a harder client-mix within that service, or simply not enough shared instances to be a fair comparison — treat it as a prompt to look closer, not a verdict.

Data source and window: SharePoint "Time Sheet" list export, ( logged entries). Same export pipeline as the monthly BTA KPI Dashboard. This is a one-off analytical report on the current data snapshot, not a recurring/auto-refreshing tool — re-run on request with fresh data.
