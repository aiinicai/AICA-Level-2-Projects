Variance Analysis

Compare each account (or customer / vendor) between two periods, year on year or any two date ranges, and list the changes that need an explanation.

Audit standard note: a substantive analytical procedure (ISA 520). The changes it lists are pointers for enquiry, not misstatements. Explanations should be corroborated with other evidence.

Ageing Analysis

Age open receivables or payables by due date as at a chosen date, by party, and see how much is overdue and for how long.

Audit note: ageing is an analytical procedure that supports recoverability (expected credit losses) for receivables and cut-off / unrecorded liabilities for payables. It only makes sense on a list of open items; use the open-item field to leave out cleared lines.

Rule Builder

Build your own tests without code: pick fields, conditions and how they combine. Matching lines can be added to the Exceptions list and the report.

A rule is a pointer for follow-up, not a conclusion.

Sample Selection

Offline audit sampling workspace for sales, purchases, expenses, journals and other transaction populations.

Audit standard note: Financial-reporting standards (IFRS / Ind AS) do not deal with audit sampling, and no auditing standard prescribes a universal sample size. Sampling here is a planning aid built around auditor judgment and the applicable auditing standards — ISA 530 (SA 530 issued by ICAI in India) with ISA/SA 320 (materiality) and ISA/SA 450 (evaluation of misstatements). Paragraph references (checked against the extant ISA 240, 450 and 530 texts): ISA 240 paras 30(c) and 33(a); ISA 530 paras 5(e), 12–15 and A3/A5; ISA 450 paras 5 and 11. Standards are revised from time to time — confirm against the edition in force for your engagement, and document your own basis for the final sample.

Journal Entry Testing

Why this page exists: ISA 240 paragraph 33(a) requires testing the appropriateness of journal entries recorded in the general ledger and other adjustments made in preparing the financial statements — including evaluating the risk of management override of controls. This page runs standard red-flag analytics for that: unusual account combinations, possible threshold-splitting/structuring and, where the fields are mapped, journals that do not balance, period-end / post-closing entries, rare users, all-round amounts and missing narrations.

Journal risk ranking

Each journal scores points for every red flag it triggers: does not balance 40; posted after period end 30; single-line, late-entered or last-days-of-period 20; unusual account combination 15; rare user, seldom-used account or future-dated 10; risk keyword or round amounts 5; no narration 3. Scores add up (maximum 100). High = 40+, Medium = 20-39, Low = below 20. The score only ranks where to look first; it is not evidence of error or fraud. Click a row to see why.

Run JE tests to see results.

2. Unusual account combinations

Each journal entry's distinct set of GL accounts is treated as a "combination." Combinations that occur rarely across the whole population — e.g. accounts that almost never get journaled together — are flagged, since an override or error is more likely to produce an unusual pairing than a routine, frequently-recurring one.

Run JE tests to see results.

4. Other ISA 240 red flags

Journals that do not balance, that are posted after (or in the last days before) the period end, that are posted by users who rarely post, that consist only of round amounts, or that carry no narration. These are indicators for follow-up, not conclusions. Each check only runs when its field above is filled in.

Run JE tests to see results.

5. Account summary

Activity by GL account: lines, journals, debits, credits, net and share of total value. Use it to spot accounts with unexpected volume, large one-sided balances, or very few postings, and to agree the totals to the trial balance (ISA 240 / ISA 520).

Run JE tests to see results.

3. Threshold-splitting / structuring

Looks for the same account being hit by two or more separate entries on the same day, each individually below your Approval threshold, that together add up to more than it — a possible sign of deliberately splitting a transaction to avoid sign-off.

Set an Approval threshold above and run JE tests to see results.
